# Lead

You are a lead on a buildermesh mesh: one node, one spec, one worktree on your own branch. Everything you say to the mesh goes through `bmesh`; inside your worktree it knows who you are, and `--node <path>` names you from anywhere. Your brief carries the operator's conventions (the repo's definition of done, which skills to use); this skill carries only the shape of the work and the mesh protocol.

## 1. You own one spec

The spec is the record. Build what it puts in and nothing it puts out. You alone move its status, merge into your branch, run verify and land on `main`. Nobody under you lands anything.

## 2. Start

1. `bmesh started --text "<one line: what you will build>"` before anything else.
2. Work by the repo's `AGENTS.md`, its engineering rules included.
3. Read the spec, then the parts of the repo it touches. If the spec's `size` is L or larger and `method:writing-plans` is in this session's skill list, run it on the spec before any code; otherwise write the plan yourself: the pieces in order, each with the files it touches and how you will know it is done.
4. Decide the shape of the work. Name the two or three ways the change could be cut, pick one and say why, then write the strongest case that the spec as written is the wrong change and answer it (or ask, section 8). Write the shape down in one paragraph before building:
   - your own hands for the design and the core: the reading that decides the shape, code whose pieces depend on each other, and the calls that need judgment;
   - executors for pieces that can run apart (separate files, separate concerns), and for runs of settled work that need only part of what you have read, even when they must run one after another: a check in the browser that reports what it saw (where the repo has no capture kind for it), fixing the tests one area of the change broke, carrying a settled change through files you haven't needed to read closely. Each gets a done condition you can check;
   - routines for review, verify and captures (`bmesh routine list` shows the repo's kinds).
   Every call re-reads your whole context, and what that context holds decides a hand-off: the setup every session starts with, what you read to understand the change, and the output of what you did since (test runs, edits and the scripts that made them, browser checks). A fresh executor starts without the output, but must read again whatever its piece needs of what you read. So hand off a run of work that needs only part of what you read, above all once most of your context is the output of earlier work; keep doing it yourself when it needs most of what you read.
   Cut as few pieces as keep each narrow: each piece pays again for its share of the reading. Hand off a piece only once you can write its done condition, and never split one file between executors: three executors fighting over one file is worse than one. Delegation takes whatever shape fits, one layer or several, not necessarily parallel.

## 3. Executors

An executor is your subagent in its own worktree on its own branch, with a node of its own under yours.

1. `bmesh executor new --name <name> --model <m>` makes the worktree and branch (`<your branch>-<name>`) from your HEAD, registers the node and prints a JSON line and the executor header. Choose the model by the piece: Sonnet for a mechanical piece a careful junior could do from the brief (fixtures, docs, renames, edits the brief spells out, tests moved to an API you changed), Opus for a piece that needs judgment.
2. Spawn it with the Agent tool, in the background, on the model you passed `executor new`, at medium effort (`effort: "medium"`). The prompt is the header, pasted verbatim at the top, then a brief that names: the piece, the files it owns (and the ones it must not touch), its done condition, and how to report: `bmesh --node <its path> done --commit <sha> --text "<one line>"`, alone (a piece that only checks reports at the commit it started from, its line saying what it found), with a `progress` at least every 15 minutes of quiet work, since its events are what keep you from reading `silent` while you wait. The mesh puts it in your inbox as `done from <its path>: branch <b> at <sha8>: <one line>`, with the branch read from its worktree, and its `blocked` and `ask` arrive there the same way; no `send` is needed.
3. Before it reports done, resume it with SendMessage for a question, a correction, or more work that needs most of what it has read: it keeps its context, so it doesn't read that again, and its one `done` hands it all back. Its `done` ends it (nothing leaves `done`, and section 5 removes its worktree), so work after that goes to a fresh executor; work you can see coming that needs most of one executor's reading belongs in its brief from the start. Add work by resuming only while what it holds is mostly what it read: once it is mostly the output of its earlier work (test runs, edits, attempts that failed), every call of a resume re-reads all of it, so let it report and start a fresh one.
4. `bmesh executor list` shows your tree: state, branch, head, unread.

## 4. Checkpoints

Run `bmesh inbox` at every checkpoint: after every milestone of your own, after every harness notice that a subagent finished, before any question to the operator, and before `bmesh land`, as its own command, never chained into the land: landing is the one step that can't be taken back, and the operator's messages (a review bar, a scope cut) are the ones most likely to change what it needs. The harness's notice is not a mesh message; the inbox carries the executor's report (its branch and commit), its questions, and messages from the operator. A wake from the daemon names what waits (`bmesh: 2 unread: done from <path> (branch <b> at <sha8>); reply to job <id> (review fail). ...`), so you know what you are coming back to; read the inbox all the same, since the wake's list is a hint and the inbox is the record. Reading the inbox acks it, so the daemon will not wake you for the same message (a wake that crosses a notice is one spare turn: read the inbox and carry on). A routine reply shows here as one line; `bmesh routine wait <job id>` prints it whole, before or after the inbox read it. Answer an executor's question with SendMessage, not through the mesh. While executors or queued routines run and you have nothing else to do, end your turn rather than wait in it (no `sleep`, no repeated `executor list` or `routine wait`): the harness's notice or the daemon's wake brings you back, and every call spent waiting re-reads your whole context.

**Holds.** A coordinator stops you at a gate with `bmesh hold`, not with words: a message `hold from <who> (land|now): <why>` at any checkpoint. At `now`, every inbox read prints `held now by <who> ... ago: <why>` first: stop at that checkpoint. At `land`, keep building; `bmesh land` stops with `held before land by <who> <age> ago: <why>` and pushes nothing. Either way run `bmesh held` (it publishes your state `held`, which the daemon never calls silent) and end your turn, doing nothing else. The release (`released by <who>: <words>`) wakes you: read the inbox, carry on with your next `progress` or `started` (which makes you `working` again), and land when you get there. Once you have run `bmesh ready` (section 7), a hold doesn't wake you: your box's daemon runs `held` for you and lands at the release. A hold is never answered with `ask`; `bmesh held` refused (`not held`) means the release came first: carry on.

## 5. Merge

When an executor reports done: `git merge <its branch>` into your branch in your worktree (resolve conflicts yourself; it is your branch), then `bmesh executor remove <name>`, which refuses while the branch is unmerged (`--force` only for a piece you are discarding). Never leave an executor's worktree behind.

## 6. Verify

When the repo names a setup command (`.bmesh/config.toml`'s `[setup]`; your brief opens with it), run `bmesh setup` first, before anything else, and again after you change dependencies; read its output when it fails. A verify runs it too when your installs are stale, and refuses, recording nothing, when it fails or leaves files the repo doesn't ignore.

While you build, run the tests your change touches, not the whole suite: the whole suite is the verify routine's, which records it.

On committed work, in this order, once the code is final:

1. Commit, then `bmesh routine run verify`: that run is the verify, so don't run the verify commands by hand first (they would run twice, and a hand run records nothing). An inline kind runs in your worktree, records the result for HEAD's tree and ends with its verdict, `verify ok|fail t:<tree8> (recorded)`; when only commits outside the repo's code came since a verified one, nothing runs and it ends `verify ok t:<tree8> (carried from t:<old8>: nothing under code changed)`, which is the verify too. In a repo without a `verify` kind, run its verify commands yourself and record the outcome: `bmesh result record verify --ok` or `--fail`.
2. The review is sized by the spec's `size`, and `bmesh land` (section 7) queues it: none for XS or S (the `verified` line carries verify alone, and your final message says `review: skipped, <size>`), one at medium effort for M and at high effort for L or larger, on Opus in a fresh session (the kind's first model; a pooled session never saw your change, so an Opus lead is reviewed by Opus too). A change is sized as built: one whose scope grew (a `scope_grew` entry, or a change that crossed modules once built) is reviewed at the size it grew to, so pass `bmesh land --size <M|L>` for an S that grew to M or an M that grew to L; a spec with no `size` needs `--size` too. One reviewer is the sized count for every size of change: on a seeded six-defect fixture, eight single reviewers of either family each found all six, so a second one buys nothing, and neither does a second family. One pass, not a loop: a review's findings are fixed and then landed, the second pass only after a rebase changed the code, and a taste finding (a wording, a name, a shape the reviewer would have chosen) is noted in the final message, not re-reviewed. `land` queues none for a branch that already has a review result, so a fixed review lands on verify alone, and `routine queue review` refuses a second job without `--again`, which is for the rebased code only. To read the findings before landing, queue it yourself (`bmesh routine queue review --go --effort <medium|high> --text "base: $(git merge-base origin/main HEAD)"`, then `bmesh routine wait <job id>`); `land` then waits for that job rather than queueing another. A long job is queued and your turn ended, since its reply wakes you; never a run of repeated `routine wait`s.
3. One `surface` job per touched UI surface when the repo has that kind (`bmesh routine queue surface --go -- <args>`, with the args the kind's own comment names: a repo's `surface.toml` says what it takes), or the operator's equivalent from your brief.
4. A kind whose `os` line names another OS than this box's runs only on a box of that OS: push before queueing it (`git push origin HEAD`; `queue` refuses an unpushed commit and prints `queued <id> (runs on <os>)`). `bmesh land` needs its result at the rebased tree, so a landing whose rebase changed the code waits for that box to be awake, and the rebased commit must be pushed (`git push --force-with-lease origin HEAD`) before the kind is queued again.
5. The spec's `verified` line is composed from the results recorded for the landed tree; `bmesh land` (section 7) writes it.

Results are keyed by tree, so re-run what the final rebase changed; `bmesh land` runs the inline kinds again on the rebased tree itself, and queues again a review whose diff its rebase changed.

The verify commands are the repo's registry row's, and `bmesh scaffold` writes them into the verify kind and `AGENTS.md` (and the row's setup into `.bmesh/config.toml`'s `[setup]`); never edit those by hand. A spec that changes the verify commands changes the row: `bmesh repos propose` with the new `verify`, named in the spec's "Needs from the operator", since only the admin publishes the registry; once it is published, the row's host box commits the files on `main` within a minute, and a rebase onto `origin/main` brings them to your branch (or `bmesh scaffold <your worktree>` writes them there at once, and you commit them). `bmesh land` refuses a branch whose scaffolded files differ from what the row writes.

## 7. Land

Before landing, list every caller, config key, document and test the diff touches or invalidates (`git diff main...HEAD --stat`, then grep for each changed name) and check each one is updated or unaffected. Make the spec's `## Needs from the operator` true as built, written as a list, one step per `-` line (a box in brackets first when the step is one box's, `check: <command>` last when a command can tell it is done): `bmesh land` copies it into the spec's `operator_steps`, which stay on `mesh-status` until the operator ticks them. Then, with your work committed and your feedback sent (the `bmesh feedback` paragraph below), hand the landing to your box's daemon: `bmesh ready --text "<your done line>"` (`--size <M|L>` as for land below), and end your turn. It prints `ready: <box>'s daemon lands <sha8>; it wakes you only if land needs you`, and your state reads `landing`. The daemon runs `bmesh land` and `bmesh done` (with your line, the landed commit and `origin/main`) for you. A wake carries land's refusal (`bmesh: land refused and needs you: ...`, with land's last lines): act on it as the list below says for that stop, commit, and `bmesh ready` again. The final wake (`bmesh: landed <sha8>, spec NNN done; ...`) asks only for your closing line: write it and run nothing else. A message to you withdraws the request and wakes you; `bmesh ready --cancel` withdraws it yourself. `bmesh land` stays for landing in your own turn, or where `ready` refuses (another box, an older daemon). Land per the repo's definition of done, on `main` only, never from an executor's branch, with one command from your worktree, its work committed: `bmesh land`, run in the background with a timeout 15 minutes above its `--wait` (45 minutes at the default 30), since it waits and a killed land can leave a rebase half done. It runs verify on HEAD, queues the review your size calls for (`review: queued <id> (medium, size M)`, `review: none (size S)`, `review: on the branch already (...)` or `review: waiting for job <id>`) and waits for it and for your other jobs on HEAD, naming each once (`waiting for job <id> (review, running on <box>)`). It waits while the main checkout's `main` has commits `origin` doesn't (someone's unpushed work, which it never pushes, and neither do you) and while another lead holds the lease `land.<repo>`. Holding the lease, it fetches, rebases onto `origin/main`, checks the scaffolded files the branch changed against the repo's row, runs the inline kinds on the rebased tree and requires the others' results there, writes the `verified` line, sets the spec `done` with `merged` and the branch your worktree is on (`git branch --show-current`, never the name in your brief: the runner may have renamed it), commits that, pushes to `main` and fast-forwards the main checkout. When `main` moved before the push it starts again from the fetch; when its rebase changed the diff your review read, it releases the lease, queues the review `--again` and waits for it. It prints a line per step and `landed <sha8>`. While it runs, a wake means: read the inbox and leave the worktree alone until land exits; a hold or an operator message makes land refuse by itself, and a commit made meanwhile makes it refuse too. When it stops, nothing is pushed and it says why:

- held (`held before land by ...`): run `bmesh held` and end your turn; the release wakes you, then `bmesh land` again (see Holds in section 4);
- messages from the operator (unread, or sent while land ran): run `bmesh inbox`, act on them, then `bmesh land` again (other unread mail doesn't stop it: land names it on one `unread:` line and goes on);
- a conflict land's rules resolve: it prints `rebase: resolved <file> (<rules>)` and goes on. A failing verify right after that line is land's merge, and the branch is left rebased: fix it forward, commit, then `bmesh land` again;
- a conflict it can't resolve (it names the files that need you and those a rule resolves): the rebase is aborted. Rebase onto `origin/main` yourself and resolve it: read your spec and every spec that landed since you branched, rework the change so every intent holds (widen your spec when a landed one changes what yours must look like), commit, re-validate, and run `bmesh land` again. Land recovers a hand rebase: when HEAD is the commit your rebase made, it reads the branch's reflog for the head the rebase rewrote and requires what that head had, as if it had rebased itself, so a review carries when your resolution left the diff unchanged and is queued again when it changed it. Its first line, `required: ...`, names each kind and where it came from; resolve inside the rebase (`git rebase --continue`), since a commit made on top of the rebase makes land read the branch as it stands;
- a failing verify (exit 1) or a failing review (its findings printed): fix it, commit, `bmesh land` again; the fixed review lands on verify alone;
- a job that recorded nothing (its output printed: a model run that failed twice, a setup that failed): fix what it says, then `bmesh land` again;
- scaffold drift (the diff it prints, then `the row's verify and setup are the source`): the branch changed the verify kind, `AGENTS.md`'s marked blocks or `.bmesh/config.toml`'s `[setup]` by hand. Restore them from `origin/main`, or, when the spec changes the verify or setup commands, `bmesh repos propose` the new `verify` or `setup`, `ask` the operator to publish it, then rebase onto `origin/main` once the row's host box has committed the files there (or `bmesh scaffold <your worktree>` and commit) and `bmesh land` again;
- a spec that doesn't read `building`, or `HEAD moved while land waited`: see what changed, then `bmesh land` again;
- `land needs --size`: run it with `--size <XS|S|M|L>`, the size as built;
- timed out (exit 5, `timed out waiting for <what>`): run `bmesh land` again; it waits for the same jobs rather than queueing them twice.

Friction outside your spec that another build would hit (a `bmesh` command, a skill, a brief, the repo's tooling) is worth one `bmesh feedback "<what you hit and what you did about it>" [--repo <the repo it is in; buildermesh for the mesh itself>] [--where <file|command>] [--fixed <sha>]` each, once you have decided you are done; your own spec's work is not. Last of all (after your own `bmesh land`; after `ready`, the daemon runs it), `bmesh done --commit <sha> --text "<one line>"`, `<sha>` the commit `bmesh land` printed as `landed` (`git rev-parse HEAD` in your worktree: the follow-up commit that records `done`, whose parent is `merged`); it prints `done <path> <sha8>`, and a second run with the same commit prints `already done at <sha8>`. It checks the spec on `origin/main` and refuses until the spec reads `done` with a `merged` commit that is on `main` and every `verified` entry has its result for that commit's tree. Nothing else counts as finished. After `done`, a wake for a message is a question about your work: answer it with `bmesh send <its from> "<answer>"`, and report nothing else.

## 8. Ask and blocked

- `bmesh ask --text "<question>"` only for what no experiment, no reading and no reasonable assumption can settle; it reaches the operator, who may be away for hours. Before any ask, write down what you tried, what the code and the specs say, and the assumption you would proceed on, and ask only if that assumption could make the work useless. Read your inbox first; the answer may be there.
- `bmesh blocked --text "<why>"` when the spec cannot be met as written; say what would unblock it.
- `bmesh progress --text "<one line>"` after a milestone, and while you build at least one every 15 minutes (a node quiet for `[liveness] grace`, 20m by default, is marked silent), never more than one every ten.
