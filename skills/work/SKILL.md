# Work

Start from the operator's problem, never from a routing word. Read it and the code, size the work, and take the first rung below that holds. Never ask which path to take, and never make the operator restate their state (testing, low on budget, away) once said: keep it for the session and name it on the first line of each reply while it holds ("Holding for your QA: ..."). The binary does the checking (the row, holds, a red `main`, a free path, the lead cap) and refuses with what to do next: act on its refusal, never redo its checks, and pass `--force` only when the operator says so. Only the operator, in this chat, starts a round or a lead: a subagent or reviewer you start never runs `bmesh ideate`, `build`, `lead start` or anything else that starts a thread. A change you hand to a subagent instead of making it yourself is its to finish: it verifies, reviews and lands it as rung 3 does and reports one line (the commit on `main` and its push state, or why not), and you read that line, not its diff.

**Name the rung.** The first message you write on a request opens with the rung's line, its words as written up to the comma or colon (each rung below gives its own), and for a change its size with one clause of evidence: written once you have read enough to decide, or, when you act before writing anything, as the first line of your report. Nothing goes before it but the QA hold's line while one holds. Say it once per request, and again only when their answer starts a rung (an ok on a plan, a pick), never on the messages that only carry on. Their words for leads in flight and releases take no rung and name none. `bmesh audit` sets each named rung beside the one the session's actions imply, so name the one you take.

**Read first.**

- **The repo**: the one named, else the one this session runs in. `bmesh repos show <repo> --json` has its path, `host` and verify.
- **What's in flight**: `bmesh mesh-status` (leads, rounds, asks, landings); for an idea or a pick, `bmesh spec list --repo <repo>` too. A spec being built reads its old status in the ledger until it lands `done`: only its lead's node says it is building or blocked. On a box with no hub (`no hub at <addr>: is bmesh serve running?`) nothing is in flight; rungs 1 to 4 work without one, and leads and rounds need `bmesh serve`.
- **The code** the problem touches, before deciding anything.

## The ladder: stop at the first rung that holds

1. **A question, or they're testing.** Open `Answering, no change`. Answer from the code and change nothing, ending on the answer: a reply that ends on a question is a plan's. While they QA or say to hold ("hold off", "don't fix yet"), leave what they're testing alone (no edit, restart, rebuild or deploy of it) and start nothing. Keep a numbered list, each item with what they saw, its cause (or `not looked at`), the fix and its size, and answer each in one line: `3 noted: <cause>; fix: <change> (XS)`. A "fix X" goes on the list as picked unless they say now. Their release ("go", "fix 1 and 3") makes the picked items as one change by rung 3, a commit each, with one verify, one review sized by the batch and one push; what wasn't picked stays listed.
2. **Already done or being built.** Open `Already being built: <the lead's path>` or `Already done: <the commit>`. Say which, with the lead's path and state or the commit on `main`, and stop. Words for a lead in flight go to it: `bmesh --node operator send <path> "<their words>"`.
3. **This session can make it: XS, S or M** (XS one file, S one module, M crosses modules). Open `Making it here, <size>: <one clause of evidence>` (`Making it here, S: only internal/land changes`). A problem reported is a change to make. No spec, spec id, lead or commit that only records state:
   1. **Where.** A worktree of your own on a branch from `origin/main` (`git -C <repo path> worktree add <dir> -b <branch> origin/main`, `<dir>` the harness's or under `~/.bmesh/worktrees/<repo>/`), never the repo's checkout. A repo with no checkout on this box is made on its row's `host`.
   2. **Make it**: the smallest change that does what was asked, by the repo's `AGENTS.md`, with the docs it keeps exact changed in the same commit. Run the tests it touches as you go.
   3. **Verify.** Commit, then `bmesh routine run verify` in the worktree until it ends `verify ok`. A repo with no `verify` kind: run its verify commands yourself.
   4. **Review** your diff (`git diff origin/main...HEAD`) by the `ponytail-review` skill, here, at your own effort. Fix its Must fix and Should fix findings once and verify again; the rest go in your report. For M, one more review: Opus at medium effort in a fresh session (a subagent), given the operator's words, the diff and the verify line, told to review by `ponytail-review` and to start nothing.
   5. **Land.** `git fetch origin`, rebase onto `origin/main` (verify again when it brought commits), `git push origin HEAD:main`; when refused because `main` moved, fetch, rebase, verify and push again. Bring the repo's checkout level when it is on `main` and clean (`git -C <repo path> merge --ff-only origin/main`), then remove your worktree and branch. Never move, reset or push commits on `main` that aren't yours.
   6. **Release** only when they want it running now and the repo is released by tag (`git tag --list 'v*'`): see Releases below.
4. **It needs their eyes first**: L (it changes an interface others depend on), a call of taste, a release or deploy they didn't ask for, or a spend past the budget they set. Open `Plan for your ok, <size>: <one clause of evidence>` (`Plan for your ok, L: changes the brief every lead reads`), then write the plan in five lines: what changes, where, how it is checked, its size, and what you won't do; end on the one question their ok answers. When a grilling skill is installed here (one named like `grill-me`), offer it in one line. Start it on their ok, here by rung 3 with the review at high effort, or by rung 5 when it is long or splits, with the plan as the spec. A change that grows to L while you make it stops there with the same five lines.
5. **Leads**, for specs they pick, pieces that run independently, or work longer than this session should carry. Open `Starting leads for 012 and 014` (one: `Starting a lead for 013`). `bmesh build <repo> <NNN> [<NNN>...] [--context -]` starts a lead per spec, three at a time per repo, on the row's `host` (on another box, run it there: `ssh -o BatchMode=yes <host> "bash -lc '<command>'"`, quoted twice, the operator's words on stdin; a host you can't reach is said and nothing starts). Their words beyond the pick ("2 smaller", "keep the old flag") go in `--context`, on stdin through a quoted heredoc. A model named for one spec ("3 on opus") is that spec's own `bmesh build <repo> <NNN> --model <m> --family <F>`, run apart, since `--model` on a batch overrides every spec's judge. Report each `warning:` it prints as printed: renaming a command is the operator's call. Specs that edit the same files go one after another. What starts together is one family: pass the first build's `--family <F>` to the later ones. A spec written from the chat for a lead lands first (`bmesh spec next-id --repo <repo>`, `bmesh spec template`, `status: proposed`, committed as `Propose NNN: <title>` and pushed). Write each lead's brief as what can be checked (outputs, refusals, tests), and name the spec or brief file in your reply before the leads start, so the operator can read it while it's cheap to change. From then on carry only the specs, the leads in flight and what to check when each reports. Every lead you start is watched, its asks relayed to the operator in their words and their answers back, and its family's feedback drained when `watch-done` names it: the `next:` lines of `build`, `watch` and `feedback list` say how.
6. **What to do next** ("what would you improve in X", "ideas for X"): open `Starting a round on <repo>`, then `bmesh ideate <repo>`, a round (one thread, or two when the app runs on another box) that returns a ranked pitch: watch it as its `next:` line says, and when it settles, give the pitch as its line carries it; one idea of theirs to be specced at length in a thread: `bmesh ideate <repo> --idea --context -`. Their answer starts the work: "1 and 3" builds those (rung 5; an XS or S line the pitch carries without a spec is made by rung 3), "2 no, <why>" declines it (`bmesh spec set --repo <repo> --id NNN --status rejected --field reason="<why>" --land`), "4 later" sets `later` the same way. A spec a live lead is building is declined only once the operator has said to stop that lead (`node stop`), since its land would bring `done` back. Map positions to the pitch they answered, the newest one they saw; when a number could mean two things, ask in one line.

**Never cut**: what the operator is running is never touched; verify; a review sized to the risk; the push state, from a fresh fetch; their say over releases, deploys and big spends.

## Leads in flight

- **Their words for a lead**, on its path as `--node operator`: "hold X" is `bmesh hold --gate now` ("hold land on X" without `--gate`; "build these, land none until I say" is `build --hold land`), "release X" for a held lead `bmesh unhold`, "stop X" `bmesh node stop`, never a message (mail wakes a thread), and "wipe X" a stop, then `bmesh node forget <path> --wipe`, `--dry-run` first.
- **Status.** "What's building" or "what's waiting on me" is `bmesh mesh-status`, one line per lead or round: asks first, then blocked, silent, held, landing and what landed since the last look; a quiet mesh is one line. A box's hold (`<box>: <repo> waits for your approval`) is the operator's: say what it holds and the `bmesh hosts approve <id>` they run in a terminal on that box, never run by you. Then `bmesh spec steps` (landed specs with operator steps open, each with the command that ticks it), and `bmesh update --notice` lines last, word for word.
- **Releases.** "Ship X", "cut a release of X", or "release X" when X is a repo and no lead of it is held, is `bmesh release <repo>` now: they asked for it. A batch of leads ends, once they are done and its feedback drained, with `bmesh release <repo>` for each repo it landed in that is released by tag. Take first any step the repo's `AGENTS.md` names before a release; a refusal says what else it needs, and a `held` or `main is red` one is reported, never forced.

## Long sessions

What lasts is each unit's spec, which lands with its code, and the landed commits; the mesh has what's building. A stream's plan and progress live in a state file that outlives the session, never committed: name its path in replies, and delete it when the stream ends, a lesson worth keeping made a rule in `AGENTS.md` first. Once your context grows, a `bmesh` command's last line says so, with what to do and how; run `bmesh context` right before executing a finished design.

End with one line: what changed or started (the commit on `main`, or the lead or round and its host), its push state from a fresh fetch, what you didn't check, and what waits on the operator, each named in plain words. The line is final and reads on its own, but for a plan's: its question comes after it, last.
